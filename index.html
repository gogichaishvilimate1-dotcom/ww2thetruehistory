Chapter 01 · The Holocaust

Millions of lives.
Never statistics alone.

The Holocaust was the systematic persecution and murder of six million European Jews by Nazi Germany and its collaborators. It developed through years of antisemitic exclusion, dispossession, forced displacement, mass shootings, deportation, and murder in ghettos and killing centres.

Nazi Germany and its collaborators also persecuted and murdered other groups, including Roma and Sinti people, disabled people, Polish and Soviet civilians, Soviet prisoners of war, and others. The experiences of these groups were distinct, but all were shaped by Nazi racial ideology, war, and state violence.

  • 01Exclusion became policy. Laws and state measures stripped Jewish people of rights and livelihoods.
  • 02Persecution became deportation. Ghettos, forced labour, and mass transports tore communities apart.
  • 03Genocide was carried out. Mass shootings and killing centres murdered millions of people.
Chapter 02 · France, 1940

Paris under
occupation.

German forces entered Paris on 14 June 1940. France’s defeat led to occupation in the north and west and to the collaborationist Vichy regime in the south. Daily life was reshaped by censorship, shortages, repression, collaboration, and resistance.

Occupation was not a single experience. People faced different choices and dangers, while Jewish residents were progressively excluded, arrested, deported, and murdered as part of the Holocaust across Europe.

  • 14 JUNGerman troops entered Paris; the French government had already left the city.
  • 22 JUNThe armistice with Germany formalized France’s defeat and divided the country’s administration.
  • 1944Paris was liberated in August as Allied and French forces advanced.
